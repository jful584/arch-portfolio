Diagramming Motion

Spring 2026

The goal of this project was to create a diagram of a specific motion that could then generate a unique and spatial model. My motion was a sprint starting from a block start on the ground. To track the motion of my head, arms, and legs, I used lights with a long exposure camera to capture movement as streaks of light, along with rotoscoping frames of my body running across a video taken simeoutaneously. The first iteration was done in a smaller room with only two lights, the second was done in a larger and darker room with two flashlights, a headlight, and painters tape producing the blue streaks. Two runs were then stitched together, one with the lgihts on my hands and another with lights on my shoes, to capture all extremeties during the motion. After capturing the motion, I diagrammed the hidden layers to represent the action in a new way. My focus was on the speed and intensity of the explosive running motion, so I wanted to capture this through a changing line thickness and a use of vector arrows. Additionally, the lower triangles diagram my stride legnth and angle differences between my foot landing and pushing off. This diagram was used to generate the model in Rhino. I explored three ascpects of the diagram. First, I looked at the space between the bounding lines and curves by lofting between them. Second, I stretched the lines and surfaces by the intensity of the motion at each point. Third, I distorted the entire motion using time by moving each point down relative to the moment in time it was captured using Grasshopper. Combining these mehtods of representation together produced the final generative model.
